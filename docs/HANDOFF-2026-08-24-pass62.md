## 1. The scale problem — your note, answered

> *"a lot of the graphs are comparing low numbers like $300 balance to a 100k
> account which should be even legible in a graph so we have to figure out a way
> to display this properly."*

**Measured first.** The active accounts span **seven million to one**:

| account | balance | height at 208px |
|---|---|---|
| Robinhood Brokerage | $70,291.75 | 208px |
| Robinhood Crypto | $36,834.64 | 109px |
| Chase Checking | $3,007.60 | 8.9px |
| Discover | −$557.62 | **1.65px** |
| Venture X | −$367.99 | **1.09px** |
| Robinhood Cash | $113.88 | **0.34px** |
| SoFi Savings | $0.10 | **0.00px** |

**Five of ten draw under one device pixel.** The chart was not showing them
small — it was not showing them.

`src/lib/magnitude-tiers.ts` is the answer and it is **general, not
bridge-specific**. Values split into tiers; each tier gets an axis scaled to its
own largest member and **states its magnification**:

    tier 0  axis $70,291.75  true scale  Brokerage 208px · Crypto 109px · Chase 8.9px
    tier 1  axis    $557.62  126×        Discover 208px · Venture X 137px · RH Cash 42.5px
    negligible (listed, not drawn): SoFi Savings, SoFi Checking
    empty:                          Chase Sapphire, Cash on Hand

Robinhood Cash goes from **0.34px to 42.5px**. Four refusals are written into the
module with their reasons: **no log scale** (money is not read logarithmically,
and a log axis has neither sign nor zero, so a credit card cannot exist on one);
**no minimum-bar floor** (it misreports size and breaks conservation); **no broken
axis**; and **no unlimited magnification** — ten cents beside $70,291.75 needs
702,917× to fill a bar, so past 1000× a value stops being a shape and becomes a
sentence.

**Applied in two places this session:**

- **The bridge.** A band the chart cannot draw gets **no rectangle** — a diamond
  marks where it happened, and a strip below redraws it on its own axis with
  **both ceilings printed** ("This row's full width is $521.42; the tallest band
  above is $39,934.90 — 77× larger"), so the factor is checkable against two
  numbers on the page instead of taken on trust.
- **The per-account and split hero charts.** ⛔ Lines **cannot** be tiered — two
  series on two scales in one frame is a dual axis, which misleads worse than a
  flat line, because the reader reads a crossing that never happened. So the
  chart names what it is failing to draw and points at the selector already above
  it: *"Too small to see beside the rest: Discover it Card (owed) $86.89. Pick it
  on its own above to read the line."*

🔴 **A finding I did not expect.** I wrote the negative case as "assets and what
is owed are comparable, so `split` discloses nothing" — and it was wrong. Assets
~$134k against ~$1.1k owed is a ratio over a hundred, so the owed line is pinned
to the baseline there too. The disclosure fires on `split` and is right to; the
test pins that rather than my assumption.

table, and `/accounts` have the same measurement and no treatment yet. The

### 2.3 `lib/attribution` — why `unexplained` is not a plug

It would be trivial to make the bridge always close: compute every band, set the
last to whatever is left. That number would be meaningless. So `unexplained` is
**subtraction and nothing else**, and its explanations are a separate CHECKED
input: `attributedCents` vs `unattributedCents`, **never clamped** — negative
means two explanations for the same money, which is a defect in the attribution
rather than in the ledger.
