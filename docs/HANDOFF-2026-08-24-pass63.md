### The engines

- **`lib/committed`** — rolls occurrences into a book. ⛔ **Money in is
  PARTITIONED, never netted.** `upcomingOccurrences` returns income and bills
  from one call, so a caller that forgot to filter would net $4,188 of salary
  against $3,211 of rent and publish a commitment of roughly nothing. Throwing
  would put the crash in a render path (pass 62 shipped one of those), so the
  inflow is counted and reported instead — `attribution.ts`'s rule.
- **`lib/runway`** — three states, not two. `unknown` is a **withheld verdict**:
  zero measured spending makes `spend ≤ income` true, so without it a ledger with
  balances and no transactions would be told its income covers its spending. Pass
  43 shipped that exact shape once. It refuses to name a number past **two
  years**, because a six-month mean and a levelled rate do not carry month-level
  precision thirty months out.
- **`lib/car-cost`** — forecasts from commitments and publishes the date its
  evidence runs out. Shares are clamped into [0, 100]: the denominator is
  `baseline + part`, so a month of net refunds would otherwise print a car
  costing 340% of spending.

## 2. The two service-level corrections

**Overdue is series-scoped, not category-scoped.** `budgetOverdue` answers per
category subtree; unioning it over the tree double-counts every series whose
category has a parent. Measured: that walk reports **$128.42** overdue where the
truth is **$64.21**, because Utilities and its Internet and Electricity children
each claimed the same two bills. Extracted `overdueForSeries`; `budgetOverdue`
now calls it, so "is this bill late?" has one implementation. Its 82 tests pass
untouched.

**A transfer is not a commitment.** The first filter was "anything but income",
which admitted eight `transfer` series on the fixture and published
**$7,530.90/month** committed against a **$4,799.17/month** total. Money moving
between his own accounts does not leave. `COMMITTED_KINDS = ["bill",
"subscription"]`; `other` is excluded too, being the catch-all where a misread
transfer lands.
